Table~\ref{tab:dataset_overview} summarizes the 395 benchmark programs in our evaluation. They span linear, non-linear, nested/multi-loops, function calls, pointer manipulations, and recursive data types such as linked lists. The suite includes both loop-only programs (\textcolor{figloop!75!black}{blue} cells, invariant generation only) and programs requiring full specifications (\textcolor{figcontract!75!black}{red} cells, loop invariants plus pre-/postconditions).

\toolname\ verified 353 of 395 programs (89.4\%) across numerical loops, pointer/struct fragments, linked lists, and \textbf{pIp} fragments adapted from an industrial navigation control codebase. The core symbolic execution algorithm does not systematically handle arbitrary recursion or external library/system calls; the LLM fallback covers some simple recursive cases (see \appref{app:cases}), and broader support is left to future work. These failures reflect programs that are difficult for both the LLM and the SMT backend. For nested and multi-loop programs, symbolic execution often cannot obtain precise loop preconditions, so the LLM must infer relations across loop phases or loops. For non-linear programs, the missing facts often require non-linear arithmetic reasoning, and in some cases helper functions or inductive definitions that SMT solvers cannot prove within the budget. Recursive data structures similarly require inductive predicates beyond the finite flattened-memory summary. External and recursive calls fail for a different reason: they fall outside the symbolic-execution fragment, leaving no exact symbolic anchor for synthesis.

Lines of code do not reliably reflect difficulty on these benchmarks --- the binding constraint is invariant complexity. \toolname\ reaches 96\% on Linear and Iteration-Free programs but drops to 77\% on Non-Linear loops and 63\% on Nested/Multi-Loops, where invariants must capture richer numerical relations, data-structure properties, and inter-loop interactions. The 0\% on External \& Recursive Call (3 programs) is the largest residual gap and is consistent with the scope discussion in \rev{\appref{sec:limitations}}.

\begin{finding}
\textbf{Finding (RQ1).} \toolname\ produces a \emph{Valid} specification for $89.4\%$ ($353/395$) of programs spanning linear/non-linear loops, memory, and linked lists; the residual failures concentrate on reasoning-heavy cases involving nested/multi-loop relations, non-linear arithmetic, recursive data structures, or external/recursive calls outside the symbolic-execution fragment.
\end{finding}
