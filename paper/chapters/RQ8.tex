
\subsection{RQ8: What are \toolname's dominant failure modes?}
\label{subsec:rq8-failure}

To separate terminal outcomes from the errors seen in the detailed logs, Fig.~\ref{fig:failure_root_causes} reports two views of an independent \texttt{gpt-5.4-mini} rerun on \textbf{\toolname-400}, executed to capture per-case failure logs. This rerun yields 321 valid cases, fewer than the 373 (93.3\%) of the main run in Table~\ref{tab:large_experiment_summary}; the gap is intentional, as we cap this rerun at 3 refinement iterations (versus up to 10 in the main run) precisely to expose more failure cases for characterization. The left panel gives the final case-level outcome, while the right panel classifies the 79 non-valid cases (the 42 \emph{No valid} plus 37 \emph{No syntax} cases) by the dominant source program feature observed in the input and failure logs. Table~\ref{tab:gpt54mini_failure_events} separately counts logged syntax events and prover failures by outcome (\emph{Timeout}, \emph{Unknown}, \emph{Failed}) and by obligation content (\emph{Loop Inv.}, \emph{Post Cond.}, \emph{Assigns}, and other verification conditions). The categories in Fig.~\ref{fig:failure_root_causes} are mutually exclusive for counting: if several features occur in one program, we assign the case to the first feature that explains the blocking obligation in the log.

The event distribution in Table~\ref{tab:gpt54mini_failure_events} shows where failed runs accumulate. Timeouts are dominated by loop invariant obligations (\(2{,}590/4{,}370=59.3\%\)), especially preservation and establishment goals over arrays or heap-backed arrays. Postcondition and \texttt{assigns} timeouts are smaller but still substantial, indicating that the hard cases are not confined to one prompt or clause type.

\rev{The inspected logs contain \emph{Timeout}, \emph{Unknown}, and \emph{Failed} outcomes, corresponding to proof timeouts, prover uncertainty, and backend or resource-level execution failures. These outcomes are not semantic counterexamples and cannot, by themselves, distinguish an incorrect generated specification from a correct but difficult proof obligation that the current verification backend does not discharge. In our manual review of the programs, generated specifications, and unresolved obligations, we more often observed candidate properties that were broadly consistent with the program but difficult for the current backend to prove, particularly for complex loop, array, and heap reasoning. This qualitative assessment does not rule out specification-synthesis errors in individual cases.} The method and verifier boundaries exposed by these observations are discussed in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

\begin{finding}
\textbf{Finding (RQ8).} \rev{Residual failures manifest predominantly as SMT timeouts on complex proof obligations. The logs cannot conclusively separate specification-synthesis errors from proof obligations beyond the current backend, although manual inspection more often points to verifier limitations.}
\end{finding}
