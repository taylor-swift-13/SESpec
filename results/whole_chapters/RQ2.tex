We evaluate correctness through three metrics:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Syntax rate (Syn.):} the percentage of generated specifications
syntactically accepted by the ACSL/Frama-C front end;
\item \textbf{Validity rate (Val.):} the percentage of syntactically correct
specifications whose generated clauses are accepted by Frama-C/WP under the
verifier configuration in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}. Pre-existing assertions
or verification targets are excluded from Val.; generated loop invariants are
valid only if they satisfy both the \textit{Base} and \textit{Preservation}
conditions;
\item \textbf{Accuracy rate (Acc.):} the percentage of valid specifications
strong enough to prove the benchmark verification goal. For pre/postconditions,
this means implying the given target assertions; for loop invariants, it further
requires the \textit{Termination} condition.
\end{itemize}
All metrics are assessed under Pass@k: a benchmark succeeds if any of up to $k$
complete pipeline attempts (including repair, verification, and refinement;
refinement iterations are not counted as separate attempts) satisfies the metric.
The Pass@1 cost measurements for the goal-independent study are reported
separately in Section~\ref{subsec:rq7-cost}.











Table~\ref{tab:performance_rates_model} shows that syntax acceptance
generally exceeds validity and accuracy, especially for weaker models on
complex data. On list-S at Pass@1, GPT-3.5-turbo falls from 70.83\% Syn. to
20.83\% Val. and 8.33\% Acc.: many linked-list annotations parse but fail
verification or do not prove the goal. Claude 3.7 Sonnet reaches
95.83\%/83.33\%/75.00\% on the same set.

Model gaps narrow on some families after multiple attempts. At Pass@5,
GPT-4o and Claude reach the same Acc. on SV-COMP (76.19\%) and NLA
(86.67\%); on numer-S they reach 82.50\% and 85.00\%. This pattern is
consistent with symbolic guidance helping a less capable model recover
useful candidates, although the table alone does not isolate that effect.
Pass@1-to-Pass@5 gains show that extra attempts recover difficult cases,
while Pass@3-to-Pass@5 gains are small on NLA, numer-S, and pIp
(\appref{app:rq2-full}).

The lower Acc. on OOPSLA and SV-COMP is associated with nested or multiple
loops, where symbolic execution may not derive a sufficiently strong loop
precondition; OOPSLA also lacks explicit loop conditions in some programs.
Frama-C cases additionally involve arrays, external calls, and recursion,
which challenge the covered symbolic-execution fragment. These mechanisms
explain why syntactically accepted annotations can still fail verification
or leave a goal unproved.

\begin{finding}
\textbf{Finding (RQ2).} Syntactic correctness is generally high, but the gap between validity and accuracy widens with benchmark complexity and narrows with model capability; \toolname\ narrows model gaps on several benchmark families and reaches most Pass@$k$ gains by Pass@3, with most residual accuracy loss on nested/multi-loop and memory-heavy families.
\end{finding}
